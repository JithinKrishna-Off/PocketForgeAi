## Lane 1 — Per-engine benchmarks on Android flagships (2024–2026)

### 1.1 Engine rankings on 8 Gen 3 / 8 Elite / D9300-class
- [published] **MNN-LLM** (arXiv:2506.10443, MMM-Asia 2024 workshop). Xiaomi 14 (SD 8 Gen 3), 4 threads CPU, Qwen2/Qwen2.5 1.5B–7B + llama3-8B, 4-bit: "decoding speeds that are 2.3x and 8.9x faster [than llama.cpp and fastllm]"; on GPU (OpenCL) "up to 7.1x faster decoding than llama.cpp, 1.7x over MLC-LLM". Their figure (Fig. 5) shows CPU decode Qwen2-7B @ctx256 ≈ 52.5 (MNN) vs ≈ 26 (llama.cpp) tok/s; llama.cpp 1.5B ≈ 8 tok/s @4T. **Caveat: llama.cpp baseline build flags not documented — 2.3x may be vs an untuned build.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10443v1 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **MNN-AECS** (arXiv:2506.19884). Xiaomi 15 Pro-class + 5 Android devices, Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B, Llama3.2-1B/3B int4, Gemma2-2B int8: decode 10.2 tok/s @8.8W (llama.cpp) vs 21.7 @8.7W (MNN) vs 20.6 @6.2W (MNN-AECS) on Android; on iPhone 12 llama.cpp 15.3 / MNN 27.6 / AECS 31.5 tok/s. **2.1x vs their llama.cpp on Android phones.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19884 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **ExecuTorch** (docs + PyTorch blog; OnePlus 12, SD 8 Gen 3; adb-measured, prompt 64 / 128 gen): Llama3.1-8B 4-bit g128 / g256 decode = 10.85 / 11.02 tok/s; Llama 2 7B = 11.55 / 11.6 tok/s; Llama3.2-3B 4-bit SpinQuant = 22.98 tok/s; Llama3.2-1B SpinQuant = 53.41 tok/s. https://android.googlesource.com/platform/external/executorch/+/refs/heads/main/examples/models/llama, https://pytorch.org/blog/executorch-beta (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **ExecuTorch paper** (arXiv:2605.08195, May 2026). Samsung Galaxy S25 Ultra (SD 8 Elite), Qwen3-0.6B / Llama3.2-1B / Phi4-Mini(3.8B), 256tok prefill+decode, 3 reps: "llama.cpp demonstrates higher decode throughput for Qwen3 and Phi4-Mini (attention more efficient for single-token decode than ExecuTorch's)"; ET Vulkan delegate "underperforms llama.cpp in prefill … tends to deliver better token generation". Table 5 has min/max ranges (exact t/s in PDF table). **On 8 Elite, llama.cpp CPU is the dense-decode engine to beat even against ET/XNNPACK+KleidiAI.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08195 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **HeteroInfer** (arXiv:2501.14794v2). SD 8 Gen 3, Llama-8B: decode 14.01 tok/s (GPU-OpenCL + NPU-QNN heterogeneous), = 1.50x vs MNN-OpenCL-class + lama.cpp, 2.53x vs "llama.cpp"-column and 1.52x vs MLC as plotted; Olivetti "first >1000 tok/s prefill and >50 tok/s decode" (1.8B/3B). Llama-3B 29.9 tok/s. **Their llama.cpp baseline on 8B decodes ≈ 5.5 tok/s — a weak-CPU baseline; the 1.50x headline for 8B is exactly at our gate.** https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14794v2 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **PowerInfer-2** (arXiv:2406.06282v2). OnePlus Ace 2 (SD 8+ Gen 1) offloaded: Qwen2-7B 2.4 tok/s vs llama.cpp ~0.5 (Mixtral-47B 2.4 vs 0.2; Llama-13B 2.8 vs 1.7). In-memory dense Mistral-7B: PowerInfer 12.4 tok/s @41.1 GB/s measured mem-bw (OnePlus 12). Up to 27.8x over baselines (via sparsity+I/O orchestration, models >DRAM). https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06282v2 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published/repo] **PowerServe** (GitHub, QNN/QPilot backends, Snapdragon-only: `--soc 8650/8750`). Claims "40 tokens/s running Smallthinker on mobile devices" via NPU speculative inference; benchmark table (SD 8 Gen 3 `8G3`): llama-3.1-8b w/ llama-3.2-1b QNN draft: prefill 468.35, decode columns 12.03 / 21.02 tok/s; qwen-2.5-3b 21.01; qwen-2-0.5b 104.53 tok/s. **NPU path needs QNN — unusable on MTK D9300+.** https://github.com/powerserve-project/PowerServe (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [published] **MLC-LLM** presence: consistently the prefill-heavy outlier that *loses* decode on CPU-adjacent dense models: 2410.03613 (below) and 2603.23640 "MLC-LLM on Adreno GPUs performs worse in prefill speed than llama.cpp on CPU"; Transformer-Lite work reports Qwen1.5-4B on 8Gen3 MLC decode 10–20 tok/s (cited in https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23640v1, accessed 2026-10-23).
- **Bottom line (lane 1):** No engine currently *claims and publishes* a straight llama-bench-2410× tg win ≥1.5x over a *tuned* llama.cpp CPU build for dense 4B–8B Q4 on 8Gen3/8 Elite/9300-class phones. Published ≥1.5x claims all rest on (a) untuned llama.cpp baselines (MNN), (b) NPU offload unavailable on MTK (PowerServe, llm.npu, HeteroInfer), or (c) mixed-precision GPU+NPU (HeteroInfer 8B = 1.5x).

### 1.2 llama.cpp on Dimensity/Mali slow — CONFIRMED
- [published] *Understanding LLMs in Your Pockets* (arXiv:2410.03613, v5 2026-02, journal-published ACMSys+): Vivo Pad3 Pro = **Dimensity 9300, Mali-G720-Immortalis MP12** (our exact GPU class): "For the two Mali GPUs [G78, G720], both prefill and **decode speeds are slower than on the CPUs**. The slowdown is especially significant in the prefill stage"; "for both Mali GPUs, both prefill and decode speeds are slower than on the CPUs… For models larger than 4B, the **prefill speed drops below 5 tokens/s on the Mali-G720**, despite it having the highest theoretical computational capability among all non-Apple devices"; root cause: "average **load-store unit utilization exceeds 90%**, with nearly all read operations being **partial-width**"; Mali-G720 ~1.5x Adreno-750 fp16 peak but "~10x slower prefill". Also: D9300 CPU = fastest non-Apple decode SoC, 10–20% behind Apple M1. https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03613 (accessed 2026-10-23). **Directly supports H3 (Vulkan full offload loses to CPU on this class) and the app's `DeviceOptimizer` GPU-99 default being harmful.**
- [measured] llama.cpp Vulkan benchmark discussion #10879: "MediaTek Dimensity 9400 | pp512 38.36 ± 15.15 | **tg128 8.92 ± 0.06** | b9ab0a4 | GPU supports coopmat but **pp512 is faster with it turned off**" (llama 7B Q4_0, -ngl 100). Mali-G57 entry: uma:1, fp16:1, warp:16, matrix cores:none. https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/10879 (accessed 2026-10-23). 8.92 tok/s on 7B Q4 via Vulkan on a D9400 (bandwidth ≈ same class as our 9300+) is consistent with ~22 GB/s effective = well under CPU.
- [measured] Vulkan-on-AMD generic mmvq shape-sensitivity issues reinforce that ggml Vulkan's mmvq decode path is brittle across drivers/shapes: #26663 (RX 9070 XT "MMUL_MAT_VEC at n=1 is 200–375× slower than MUL_MAT at n=128"), #26795, and historic #24066 (b9460 regression) — pattern: Vulkan decode regressions are recurring, untracked. https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/26663 (accessed 2026-10-23).
- [anecdote] llama.cpp Mali-G57 log in #10879 confirms uma/warp16/no-matrix classes (same probe shape as our phase0 Mali-G720 Immortalis: uma=1, fp16=1, warp=16, int_dot=0, matrix cores=none — FINDINGS.md §1). https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/10879 (accessed 2026-10-23).

## Lane 4 — Older systems work that transplants to phones

Note: the coordinator's "SMEM/DMU/no-KV APIs" phrasing does not map to a single recognizable source (searches: no telephony/Android "SMEM/DMU API" in the LLM context). Interpreted as the *structural mechanisms* rather than named APIs: (a) shared-memory/DMU-style persistent object pools; (b) KV-free state APIs (SSM/linear attention); (c) OS I/O + prefetch + residency controls. Both "exact technique" and "what's structurally the same on phones" given per row.

- **Linux readahead / madvise WILLNEED / fadvise** — [published, OS docs] manual page semantics: `posix_fadvise(WILLNEED)` initiates readahead (non-blocking); `madvise` MADV_WILLNEED/MADV_SEQUENTIAL adjust the window. https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/posix_fadvise.2.html, https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/madvise.2.html (accessed 2026-10-23). *Structurally same on phones:* GGUF weights are mmap'd from /data; Android's page-cache eviction pressure (our device: Cached 5.96 GB, MemAvailable 7.66 GB) interacts with LLAMA_CPP's mmap-backed weights: `madvise(MADV_WILLNEED)` on the mapped file at load + `mincore()` probe for "weight pages resident" is the same technique 1:1 (no root needed). No published on-phone measurement → **GAP**.
- **DMA/streaming model reads** — [published] LLM-in-a-flash's "parallelized reads, utilizing the inherent parallelism within storage stacks and flash controllers, using **32KiB or larger random reads across multiple threads**" = the direct descendant of server DMA/chunked nets (the same shape as FlexGen's tiered weight streaming). https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514v3 (accessed 2026-10-23). *Structurally same on phones:* UFS4.0 supports deep queues + parallel streams; PowerInfer-2's I/O-aware orchestration is the on-phone implementation of it. On a 4B-in-DRAM plan it is irrelevant to decode t/s; matters only if any weight class (embedding, lm-head) is demoted to flash.
- **Flash residency hints** — [published, OS docs] `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` / `madvise(MADV_DONTNEED)` as a *residency control* — drop clean file pages from cache to keep active weights protected from the failing page cache under memory pressure. https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/posix_fadvise.2.html (accessed 2026-10-23). On phones: page-cache of the *other* app files ("Cached 5.96 GB") is the competitor for RAM; an app that would otherwise be killed by `lowmemorykiller` (our exit=137 sim) can be arranged to be evicted first. Nothing published for LLM apps on Android → **GAP**.
- **Dialog/pred prefetch** — [published analogy] "Predictive prefetch" in OS literature (DRAM-page SLL readahead etc.) and dialog-turn prediction = prediction of future *user* action.
